What is Mine a Mountain?

A Roblox incremental mining sim where you climb, dig, freeze, and cash out — then do it again on a brand new peak an hour later.

Mine a Mountain, developed by 10K Steps, drops you at the base of a procedurally shaped mountain with a pickaxe in hand. The loop is simple to learn and hard to put down: climb, mine crystals out of the rock, watch your warmth drain as you gain altitude, and head back down before the cold ends your run.

The Core Loop

  • Climb — work your way up the mountain's slope toward rarer mining ground.
  • Mine — break rock and pull crystals into your backpack as you go.
  • Manage warmth — the higher you climb, the colder it gets. Run out of warmth and your climb ends.
  • Sell or keep — return to the lobby, place your best luck-boosting crystals on your base, and sell the rest for cash.
  • Upgrade — spend that cash on a better pickaxe, more warmth, extra carry capacity, or bombs, then head back up.

What Makes Each Run Different

The mountain itself resets on an hourly timer, so the exact shape and vein layout of the climb changes throughout the day even though the zone structure and crystal rules stay consistent. That keeps route-finding relevant instead of something you memorize once and never think about again.

Digging Luck tip: liking the game and joining its Roblox group is advertised as a permanent Digging Luck bonus — worth doing before your first real climb.

Beginner Guide

The first-hour loop, in the order it actually pays off.

Your First Climb

  • Grab your starting pickaxe and head straight up the visible slope — don't worry about finding a "perfect" route yet.
  • Mine everything in reach on the way up. Early crystals are common, but they're still your first source of cash.
  • Watch your warmth bar, not the clock. It's the real limit on how high a fresh character can safely go.
  • Turn back before you freeze. A half-finished climb that gets sold is worth more than a frozen one that gets nothing.

Spend Order That Works

Warmth first, pickaxe second is the general rule of thumb in the community. Extra warmth is what actually unlocks new mining ground — a strong pickaxe is wasted if you can never survive long enough to reach the crystals it's meant for.

  • 1. Warmth — lets you push into higher, rarer zones without freezing.
  • 2. Pickaxe — clears blocks faster once you can actually reach good ground.
  • 3. Carry Weight / Backpack — stretches each climb so you're not returning to sell every few minutes.
  • 4. Bombs — situational, but useful once your basics are solid.
Selling tip: don't sell every crystal automatically — pull out your best luck-boosting finds and place them at your base first, since they compound your future rare-crystal odds.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Chasing the summit too early. A frozen climb with nothing sold is a wasted trip — build warmth in stages instead.
  • Upgrading the pickaxe before warmth. A faster pickaxe doesn't help if you can't survive long enough to use it.
  • Selling every crystal. Skipping luck boosters slows down your long-term rare-crystal odds.
  • Ignoring the hourly reset. Routes don't carry over between mountains, so re-scan the slope each reset instead of running on autopilot.

Playing With Others

Climbing in a group is generally worth it: shared presence on a mountain is treated as improving rare crystal odds for everyone nearby, so grouping up for summit pushes tends to beat soloing once you're past the very early game.

Maximize Your Runs

How to play with intention once the basics click — upgrade math, session efficiency, and the habits that separate a fast climber from a slow one.

The Optimization Mindset

Maximizing runs isn't about grinding harder — it's about spending less time doing things that don't earn crystals. Every minute spent walking back down empty-handed, freezing on a climb you couldn't survive, or holding cash you're not spending is a minute that isn't compounding your progress.

The players who climb fastest tend to follow the same three habits: upgrade just ahead of where they're stuck, sell in batches instead of constantly, and treat their base as a long-term investment rather than a trophy shelf.

Upgrade Priority, In Depth

The beginner order — Warmth → Pickaxe → Carry → Bombs — still holds, but the more useful skill is recognizing when to switch lanes instead of following the order blindly.

A simple breakpoint test

Before every purchase, ask: "does this let me reach ground I currently can't, or does it just make ground I already farm slightly faster?" Reaching new ground almost always wins — new zones have better crystals per swing than any speed boost on old ground.

Session Efficiency

  • Batch your selling. Fill your backpack close to capacity before heading down — half-empty trips waste climbing time you already paid for.
  • Buy upgrades in one trip. Spend everything you're going to spend before heading back up, rather than trickling purchases across multiple return trips.
  • Learn the reset rhythm. If you know roughly when the hourly reset lands, time your last climb of the hour so you're not left mid-route when the mountain changes.
  • Don't over-explore low zones. Once High Ridge feels easy, treat lingering there as wasted time — push the route higher instead of fully clearing ground you've already out-leveled.

Luck & Base Strategy

Your base is a compounding investment, not a display case. Every strong crystal you place there is permanently working for you on every future climb, while a sold crystal only pays out once.

Base-building tip: aim to fill at least one base slot per rarity tier before you start being selective — a spread across tiers boosts luck more evenly than five duplicates of the same rarity.

Group Strategy for Endgame

Once Summit Approach and Summit Peak are your farming grounds, soloing starts to cost you real crystals. Grouping concentrates player presence, which is treated as improving rare-crystal odds for everyone nearby — so coordinated summit pushes with friends or an active server consistently outperform solo runs at that stage.

Efficiency Checklist

  • Warmth covers my target zone with a small buffer, not just barely enough.
  • Backpack rarely fills before I reach a natural turnaround point.
  • Base has at least one crystal per rarity tier before I start being picky about upgrades.
  • I sell and buy in one trip instead of trickling back and forth.
  • I climb with others once I'm regularly reaching Summit Approach or higher.

Zones & Altitude

Every mountain is split into the same four altitude bands. Rarer crystals live higher up — and so does the cold.

Base Camp
The lobby and starting ground. Warm, safe, and where you sell crystals, buy upgrades, and place luck boosters. No cold pressure here.
High Ridge
The first real mining stretch above camp. Rare crystals begin appearing here — a solid warmth upgrade or two is usually enough to farm it comfortably.
Summit Approach
Noticeably colder, with epic-tier crystals in the mix. This is where warmth investment starts to matter more than pickaxe power.
Summit Peak
The top of the mountain. Legendary and mythic crystals are advertised here, but only reachable with strong warmth and a bit of luck on your side.

Zone Planning at a Glance

ZoneCold PressureBest Crystal Tier
Base CampNone—
High RidgeLowCommon – Rare
Summit ApproachModerateRare – Epic
Summit PeakSevereLegendary – Mythic
Route planning: use altitude to decide risk — a higher route means better crystals but a shorter window before you have to turn back.

The Hourly Reset

A new mountain generates every hour. The zone bands and crystal rules described above hold steady between resets, but the exact shape of the slope and where veins sit will shift, so a route you memorized last hour won't map perfectly onto the next one.

Crystals

The whole economy runs on what you dig out of the rock. Sell for cash, or keep the good ones for luck.

Crystals are the main resource in Mine a Mountain. Everything you mine can either be sold back at Base Camp for cash, or — for your best finds — placed on your base as a permanent luck booster that improves the odds of finding rarer crystals on future climbs.

Luck Boosters

Placing crystals at your base rather than selling them all is what compounds your progress over time. A base lined with good boosters will noticeably shift the odds of your next climb turning up something rare.

How to decide what to keep

A simple rule: sell duplicates and common finds immediately, but hold onto your single best crystal from each climb until your base has a solid spread across rarities — then start being pickier about what earns a spot.

Pickaxes

Your pickaxe decides how fast you clear rock and how much ground you cover per swing.

Pickaxes are bought at the Pickaxes shop near Base Camp and come in increasing rarity tiers. Two stats matter most:

  • Mine Size — how much rock a single swing clears. Bigger mine size means fewer swings to clear the same patch.
  • Power — how quickly you break through tougher blocks, especially higher up the mountain where rock gets denser.

Mine Size vs. Power

StatHelps WithPrioritize When
Mine SizeClearing wide patches fastYou're farming open, low-density ground
PowerBreaking dense, high-altitude rockBlocks are visibly slowing you down near the summit

Upgrade Priority

A pickaxe upgrade is most valuable once your warmth already lets you reach a zone comfortably — at that point, more mine size and power directly translates into more crystals per climb. Upgrading a pickaxe before you can survive in a zone is the classic beginner mistake.

Rule of thumb: if mining feels slow but you're not freezing, it's pickaxe time. If you're freezing before you can mine much at all, it's warmth time.

Upgrades & Bombs

Warmth, Carry Weight, and Bombs — the three shops that shape how far and how long each climb goes.

Warmth
Backpack
Bombs

Warmth

Warmth upgrades raise the altitude you can survive at before the cold forces you back down. Since rarer crystals sit higher on the mountain, warmth is functionally the upgrade that unlocks new content rather than just making existing content easier.

Carry Weight (Backpack)

Backpack upgrades increase how many crystals you can hold before you're forced to head back and sell. A bigger backpack means longer climbs and fewer trips down the mountain, which adds up over a play session.

Warmth vs. Carry — which first?

If your runs end from freezing, warmth is the bottleneck. If your runs end from a full backpack while you're still warm, carry capacity is the better next purchase.

Bombs

Bombs are explosives bought from a separate shop, each with a different effect on the rock around you. They're more situational than warmth or carry upgrades, but useful once your fundamentals are covered — for clearing dense patches quickly or opening up ground your pickaxe alone would take a while to reach.

Spending order: Warmth → Pickaxe → Carry Weight → Bombs is a safe general order for new players, adjusted based on whichever bar (warmth or capacity) is actually cutting your runs short.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything players ask most, grouped by topic. Click a question to expand it.

General

Do I lose progress when the mountain resets?
No — the hourly reset only reshapes the mountain itself. Your cash, upgrades, and base crystals carry over between resets, so nothing you've earned is lost.
What actually ends a climb?
Two things: running out of warmth (freezing) or filling your backpack. Either one forces you back down, so managing both is the core skill of the game.
Is there a benefit to liking the game or joining its group?
Yes — it's advertised as granting a permanent Digging Luck bonus, which is worth claiming before your first serious climb since it applies to every run afterward.
Is the game still being updated?
Mine a Mountain has received regular updates since launch, so specific numbers like prices and drop rates can shift over time — treat this wiki's figures as general guidance rather than exact values.

Zones & Crystals

Why does my route feel different every hour?
The mountain's shape and vein layout regenerate hourly. The four zone bands and their rarity rules stay consistent, but the exact terrain shifts, so routes don't carry over between resets.
Should I sell every crystal I find?
No — keep your strongest finds and place them at your base as luck boosters. Selling everything gets you cash now but slows down your long-term rare-crystal odds.
Can I reach Summit Peak early on?
Technically yes, but without enough warmth you'll freeze before mining much. It's more efficient to build up warmth through High Ridge and Summit Approach first.

Gear & Upgrades

Should I upgrade warmth or my pickaxe first?
Warmth first. It unlocks entirely new zones to mine, while a pickaxe upgrade only speeds up ground you can already survive on. See Maximize Your Runs for the full priority breakdown.
Are bombs worth buying early?
Usually not right away. Bombs are more of a time-saver once your warmth, pickaxe, and backpack fundamentals are already solid.
What's the difference between mine size and power?
Mine size determines how much rock a single swing clears; power determines how fast you break through denser, higher-altitude rock. See the Pickaxes page for when to prioritize each.

Strategy & Multiplayer

Is it better to play solo or in a group?
Solo is fine early on, but grouping becomes clearly better once you're farming Summit Approach or Summit Peak, since more nearby players is treated as improving rare-crystal odds for everyone.
What's the single biggest time-waster for new players?
Trickling back and forth — selling a few crystals, buying one upgrade, climbing again. Batching your selling and buying into one trip saves a surprising amount of time over a session.
How do I know when I'm ready to push a higher zone?
If you're consistently returning with a full backpack and warmth to spare in your current zone, you're ready to push higher. If you're freezing before filling up, invest in warmth first.
